    Usage patterns

    Usage patterns are small, reusable examples for things that come up again and again when a catalysis dataset is described: a synthesis with its precursors, a measurement with a specific technique, a catalytic test in a fixed bed, a simulation and its results. Each pattern states its purpose, shows a real use case and the fields involved, and gives the example three ways: as a graph, as YAML (the format you write) and as RDF/Turtle (what a knowledge graph receives).

    Every example is an excerpt of one of the community datasets and is validated against the current schema whenever the documentation is built, so it can be copied as a starting point. For why the schema is structured this way, see Design Patterns; for every field, see the Schema Reference.

    Legend of the graphs:

    Dataset Activity (synthesis, characterization, simulation, reaction) Plan (method, technique, protocol) Entity (sample, catalyst, precursor, chemical) Agent (instrument, reactor) Quantity (value or range with unit) Qualitative value or calculated property Vocabulary term Literal value

    Boxes show an object's title (or id) and, in «guillemets», its class. Arrows are labelled with the field that links two objects. Descriptions are left out of the graph; the YAML tab shows the full excerpt.

    Section 1: Describing a dataset

    Everything starts with a CatalysisDataset: it says what the data is, who made it, which catalysis field it belongs to, and which activities produced it.

    Pattern 1 – A dataset, its classification and its activities

    Purpose: Make a dataset findable and classifiable: a persistent identifier, a title, the catalysis research field as a vocabulary term, the people behind it, the publication that uses it, and where the files are.

    Example use case: A deposit of thermocatalytic NH₃ decomposition data. The research field is heterogeneous catalysis (VOC4CAT:0007001); the dataset was produced by, among other activities, a temperature-programmed reduction, linked under was_generated_by.

    Notes:

    • activity_designator tells software which kind of activity an entry of was_generated_by is (Synthesis, Characterization or Simulation). It is needed whenever activities are nested inside a dataset record.
    • is_referenced_by links the dataset to the article; the article usually cites the dataset only by its handle, so this is the way back.

    Fields used: rdf_type, keyword, creator, name, publisher, is_referenced_by, dataset_distribution, access_URL, licence, was_generated_by, activity_designator, carried_out_by, evaluated_entity, realized_plan, plan_designator

    Section 2: Catalyst synthesis

    A Synthesis takes precursors (had_input_entity), follows a preparation method (realized_plan) and produces catalyst samples (had_output_entity). The method's class decides which parameters it can carry.

    Pattern 2 – A synthesis with precursors, amounts and a typed preparation method

    Purpose: Record what went into a catalyst, how much of it, and the conditions of the preparation method as structured values.

    Example use case: Co-precipitation of a Cu–Al–Ga hydrotalcite precursor from three metal nitrates at pH 9, aged at 65 °C and calcined. The plan declares itself a CoPrecipitation (plan_designator: voc4cat:0007795), which makes the precipitation, ageing and calcination fields available.

    Notes:

    • plan_designator takes the class_uri of the method exactly as written in the schema (voc4cat:… in lower case). Without it, the method fields still validate as JSON but cannot be loaded into the Python datamodel.
    • Some quantity fields take a list even when there is one value (e.g. has_ph_value), others a single value (e.g. has_aging_duration). The schema reference shows which.

    Fields used: activity_designator, nominal_composition, catalyst_measured_properties, had_input_entity, precursor_quantity, had_output_entity, realized_plan, plan_designator, precipitating_agent, has_ph_value, has_aging_temperature, has_aging_duration, has_calcination_temperature_range, has_calcination_atmosphere
