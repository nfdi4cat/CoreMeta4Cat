    Working with Data

    The rest of this documentation explains CoreMeta4Cat field by field. This section does the opposite: it shows CoreMeta4Cat applied to real data. Use it to see what a finished record looks like, and as a source of examples to copy when you describe your own dataset.

    • Community datasets

      Six published datasets from Repo4Cat, each described end to end: heterogeneous catalysis, microkinetics, DFT and machine-learned potentials, and biocatalysis. Download each record as YAML, JSON or RDF.

    • Usage patterns

      Small examples for recurring tasks: a synthesis with its precursors, a measurement with a specific technique, a test in a fixed-bed reactor, a simulation and its results. Each one is shown as a graph, as YAML and as RDF, and is taken from a community dataset.

    How a CoreMeta4Cat record is organised

    Every record answers the same handful of questions, in this order:

    1. What is this dataset, and who made it? A title, a description, the research field, the people who created it, and where the original files live (id, title, description, rdf_type, creator, dataset_distribution).
    2. What activities produced the data? Was a catalyst synthesized (Synthesis)? Was something measured (Characterization)? Was something computed (Simulation)? These go under was_generated_by.
    3. What catalytic reaction is the dataset about? The reaction being studied, with its reactants, catalysts, reactor, conditions, and how products were identified. This goes under is_about_activity.
    4. What catalyst or material is the dataset about? This goes under is_about_entity.

    A simple dataset might need only some of these; a pure simulation study, for example, has no reaction. Usage pattern 1 shows the dataset level in detail.

    Every activity follows the same pattern: what happened (the activity), how it was done (the plan in realized_plan, e.g. a preparation method or a characterization technique), with what (carried_out_by, e.g. an instrument) and on what (evaluated_entity, e.g. a catalyst sample). Design Patterns explains why the schema is built this way.
